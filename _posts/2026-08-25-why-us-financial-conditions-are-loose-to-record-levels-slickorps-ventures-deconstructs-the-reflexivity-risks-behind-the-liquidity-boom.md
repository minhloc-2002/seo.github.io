If one looks only at the US Treasury market today, it is hard to conclude that "money is easy to obtain." On August 18, the yield on the US 30-year Treasury briefly rose to around 5.34 percent, the highest level since 2007, with long-term real rates also at multi-year highs. Yet at the same time, the US financial conditions index has climbed into its loosest territory since the 1990s. This seemingly contradictory combination is precisely what Slickorps Ventures believes deserves the most attention. The financial conditions index is not equivalent to the federal funds rate, nor to the amount of cash in the banking system. The San Francisco Fed, in its introduction to the US financial conditions index, notes that the index aggregates eight indicators from the money, bond, and equity markets. In other words, equity valuations, credit spreads, market volatility, and funding conditions can all offset the tightening effect of high risk-free rates. The Chicago Fed NFCI, a separate index covering 105 risk, credit, and leverage indicators, stood at -0.55 as of August 7, indicating that overall financial conditions remain clearly accommodative. So what is truly distinctive about the US right now is not "low rates," but rather asset prices strong enough and a credit environment wide enough to make a high-rate economy behave like a low-friction funding environment.

## This Round of Easing Was Not Manufactured by the Central Bank Alone; the Market Itself Has Formed a Positive Feedback Loop
This raises an even more important question: if the Fed has not cut rates aggressively, why can financial conditions remain so loose? The answer likely lies in the reflexivity of the market itself. Rising stocks increase corporate market capitalization, lowering the cost of equity financing and M&A. Narrowing credit spreads allow companies to issue bonds relatively easily even against a higher Treasury benchmark. Low volatility encourages funds to increase risk exposure and leverage, with new capital continuing to flow into equity and credit markets, further pushing up asset prices. The recent Bank of America global fund manager survey shows that cash allocations fell to a multi-year low of 3.5 percent in August, while global equity allocations rose to their highest since November 2021, and fund manager sentiment reached its third most optimistic level since 2022. For Slickorps Ventures, this explains why stocks, gold, and credit assets can all remain elevated in the same period: what the market is receiving is not necessarily "central bank liquidity" in the traditional sense, but rather a market-endogenous liquidity generated jointly by asset appreciation, funding convenience, and risk-taking appetite. Rising prices improve financial conditions, and improved financial conditions support higher prices. This is itself a positive feedback system that can persist for a sustained period.

## AI Makes This Loop Stronger, but Also Concentrates Risk in the Most Capital-Intensive Areas
What truly warrants caution is that current loose financial conditions happen to coincide with the historic AI capital expenditure cycle. Reuters data shows that major AI investors such as Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta have conducted nearly 220 billion US dollars in borrowing from 2026 to date, while data centers, power, semiconductors, and network infrastructure continue to absorb capital. Meanwhile, the US 30-year real yield has approached roughly 3 percent, near an 18-year high. This creates a very peculiar structure: AI companies can borrow money, but borrowing itself is becoming more expensive; capital markets are willing to fund growth, but more and more growth now depends on continuous financing to be completed. Even more noteworthy, Reuters has previously reported that the US equity rally has been accompanied by increased financing demand from leveraged ETFs, options, and hedge funds, with bank exposure to stock buyback financing exceeding 220 billion US dollars. Slickorps Ventures therefore will not simply say "AI is the next bubble." A more accurate assessment is that AI has become one of the most capital-intensive asset clusters in the current financial easing cycle, and one of the most vulnerable to marginal changes in funding conditions. When earnings growth is fast enough, this leverage can be absorbed by cash flow; once expectations for capital returns decline, high financing needs can suddenly transform from a growth tool into balance sheet pressure.

## Slickorps Ventures Assessment: The Real Danger Is Not That Financial Conditions Are Too Loose, but That the Market Begins to Assume They Will Stay Loose Forever
In historical charts, the Gulf War, LTCM, the global financial crisis, the 2011 sovereign rating shock, and Covid-19 all left sudden downward fault lines in financial conditions. But these historical events cannot mechanically prove that "record looseness inevitably leads to crisis." What truly deserves attention is another layer of mechanism: the looser financial conditions are, the more market participants design positions, capital expenditures, and leverage on the premise that "future funding will remain ample." Consequently, the system becomes increasingly sensitive to a sudden tightening of financial conditions. August 2026 has already shown this tension. Long-term Treasury yields rose rapidly, and the semiconductor index fell about 5.6 percent in a single day on August 18, indicating that even while overall financial conditions remain loose, duration and AI assets are already reacting violently to rising capital costs. Slickorps Ventures therefore prefers to define the current environment as a "high-sensitivity market under loose conditions": the risk does not necessarily come from liquidity disappearing today, but from the fact that more and more assets are already priced on the assumption that liquidity will not disappear.
