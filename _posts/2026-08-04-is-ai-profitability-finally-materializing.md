## Slickorps Ventures Reassesses the Quality and Concentration Behind the 71% Earnings Beat

![Slickorps Ventures](https://jpcdn.it/img/r/553/367/42c44268469a8fd72b55695b9eb11df4.png)

Interim financial reports for Q2 2026 show that AI-related companies delivered earnings significantly above prior market expectations. According to data cited by Kobeissi Letter, at the time, S&P 500 companies that had reported earnings beat expectations by approximately 27%, the Nasdaq 100 by about 55%, and Bloomberg AI Value Chain-related companies by roughly 71%. These figures represent the extent to which actual earnings exceeded analyst forecasts, not year-over-year profit growth, nor shareholder returns.

As of July 31, FactSet data shows that the overall S&P 500 earnings surprise rate rose to 31.4%, with 86% of reporting companies beating EPS estimates. Slickorps Ventures believes this indicates that the AI rally is transitioning from a valuation narrative to an earnings validation phase. However, the 71% figure remains a mid-earnings-season signal and should not be directly interpreted as evidence that the entire value chain is generating profits of equal quality.

## AI Is Shifting From a Software Theme to an Industrial Revenue Chain

AI demand is no longer reflected solely in software subscriptions or advertising growth. The expansion of large model training and inference will drive cloud companies to increase capital expenditures, which in turn translates into orders for chipmakers, storage, servers, networking, power, and data center companies.

The Bloomberg AI Value Chain Index covers cloud computing, semiconductor, and hardware-related companies, reflecting exactly this kind of capital transmission. The spending of one segment becomes the revenue of another segment. FactSet data from the same period shows that 77% of reporting S&P 500 companies beat revenue expectations, indicating that earnings improvement is not solely driven by cost cutting.

However, Slickorps Ventures also emphasizes that beating revenue expectations alone cannot prove that AI demand contributed to all growth. Currency fluctuations, energy prices, M&A, pricing power, and lower analyst hurdles can all influence the results. A more accurate assessment is that AI has begun to confirm revenue and profits across multiple physical segments, but free cash flow and return on capital still require further validation.

## Behind the Dazzling Numbers, One-Off Gains Must Be Excluded

FactSet notes that the second-quarter earnings beat of 31.4% for the S&P 500 was significantly influenced by large investment gains at Alphabet and Amazon. GAAP earnings of Alphabet include approximately $98 billion in net securities gains, while Amazon recognized about $53.4 billion in non-operating pre-tax income, primarily from its investment in Anthropic.

Excluding these two companies, the S&P 500 earnings beat would drop to 9.2%, and year-over-year earnings growth would fall from 47.4% to 28.8%. The adjusted figures remain strong, but they change the quality of the conclusion.

Slickorps Ventures believes investors must distinguish among three types of profit: recurring operating profit driven by product demand, earnings surprises resulting from low market expectations, and one-off accounting gains from asset revaluation. Only the first type comes closest to the operating capability that can sustain valuations over the long term.

## The Next Phase Tests Not Demand, but Who Can Retain Profits

Strong earnings reports have not been rewarded with commensurate stock price gains. FactSet data shows that companies reporting positive earnings surprises rose an average of just 0.1% in the four days around their reports, below the roughly 1% average over the past five years. The market is no longer satisfied with merely beating expectations; it is now asking whether capital expenditures can translate into free cash flow and capital returns.

The assessment of Slickorps Ventures is that the second quarter of 2026 indeed proves AI earnings are materializing, but competition has entered a stricter phase: who holds pricing power, who must keep expanding investment, and who can retain profits after depreciation, electricity, financing, and competitive costs.

The entire AI value chain may grow, but the number of companies that can truly achieve high returns over the long term will not be equally large. The next round of valuation divergence will be determined by profit quality, cash flow, and capital efficiency, not by the label of being "AI-related."
